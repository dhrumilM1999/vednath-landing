## 16. Legal & Compliance Notes for Medical Website Content
> I'm not a lawyer. Please have the clinic check these points with a legal or compliance advisor.

- **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954:** India restricts advertising that claims to cure or treat a scheduled list of conditions. To my knowledge the list includes cancer, diabetes, high and low blood pressure, obesity, infertility, sexual-performance problems and others. The board and the current website list many of these. **Safer wording:** "Ayurvedic management of / supportive care for…", without promising a cure.
- **Cancer:** Present it only as "supportive / complementary Ayurvedic care alongside the patient's oncologist", never as a cure.
- **COVID-19 and mucormycosis** (both on the pamphlet): Mucormycosis is a life-threatening fungal infection that needs urgent antifungal treatment and often surgery. The Ministry of AYUSH has acted against misleading COVID-cure claims in the past. **Recommendation:** Leave mucormycosis off the website. Describe COVID only as "post-COVID recovery / rehabilitation support".
- **"Every disease"** (pamphlet headline): Replace it with something like "a wide range of chronic and lifestyle conditions".
- **Testimonials:** Avoid "100% cured" and "completely cured" claims. Get each reviewer's consent before publishing their name. Better still, embed or link the live Google reviews.
- **Doctor details:** Show the qualification and registration number of each practising doctor (the norm under ASU practitioner regulations, from the NCISM, the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine).
- **Disclaimer:** Add a site-wide medical disclaimer ("Results vary from person to person. This website does not replace a consultation…").
- **Privacy:** If you add a booking form, publish a privacy policy (India's DPDP Act, 2023) and don't collect more health data than needed.
